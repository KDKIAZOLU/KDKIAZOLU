Revisit when…
  • The family moves again
  • The student moves up a school level (the feeder school counts as school of origin)
  • The student becomes permanently housed: they may stay in the school of origin for the rest of that school year, no new BID needed
Formal BID meeting (liaison leads) Presume the school of origin is in the student's best interest and give priority to the parent's or youth's request. Consider student-centered factors:
  • Impact of moving on achievement, education, health and safety
  • Time left in the school year; age and grade; IEP / 504 / EL services
  • Attendance, relationships, the student's wishes; travel time (not cost)
Decide
Decision
  • Agree: enroll in the chosen school; transportation to the school of origin if chosen; supports continue
  • School system decides against the parent's or youth's request: written explanation and notice of the right to appeal. The student stays in the requested school, with transportation, while the dispute is resolved.

Law and regulation

  1. 42 U.S.C. § 11432(g)(3)(A)–(B): school of origin vs. local school; presumption for the school of origin unless contrary to the parent's or youth's request; student-centered factors; written explanation and right to appeal.

  2. 42 U.S.C. § 11432(g)(3)(A)(i)(II): a student who becomes permanently housed may stay in the school of origin for the rest of the academic year. (Same link.)
  3. 42 U.S.C. § 11432(g)(3)(C), (E): immediate enrollment; enrollment in the requested school while a dispute is resolved. (Same link.)
  4. 42 U.S.C. § 11432(g)(3)(I): school of origin includes preschool and the designated receiving (feeder) school at the next grade level. (Same link.)
