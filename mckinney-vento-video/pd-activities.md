# McKinney-Vento PD: Group Activities

A facilitator guide for a 60–75 minute session built around the video *McKinney-Vento-CitySchools-PD.mp4*. Each activity stands alone, so you can pick the ones that fit your time. The legal points in the answer keys match the video and `SOURCES.md`.

| # | Activity | When | Time | Group size |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Agree / Disagree Line-Up | Before the video | 10 min | Whole group |
| 2 | Guess the Number | Before the video | 5 min | Tables |
| 3 | Notice · Refer · Protect note-catcher | During the video | 4 min (video) | Individual |
| 4 | Who Qualifies? Card Sort | After the video | 15 min | Tables of 3–5 |
| 5 | Barrier Busters | After the video | 15 min | Tables of 3–5 |
| 6 | The Right Words: Role-Play | After the video | 10 min | Groups of 3 |
| 7 | School Action Map | Closing | 10 min | School teams |
| 8 | 3-2-1 Exit Ticket | Closing | 5 min | Individual |

**Materials:** printed scenario cards (Activities 4–6), sticky notes, chart paper and markers, "Agree" and "Disagree" signs on opposite walls, the name of each school's McKinney-Vento liaison.

**Facilitator note:** some participants have experienced housing loss themselves. Open by saying that no one has to share personal stories, and keep the focus on what staff can do.

---

## 1. Agree / Disagree Line-Up (before the video)

Read each statement aloud. Participants stand along the wall between "Agree" and "Disagree." Ask two or three people to explain where they're standing. Don't give answers yet. Come back to the same statements after the video and see who moved.

1. A student living with their aunt's family because they were evicted counts as homeless under federal law.
2. A school can wait to enroll a student until the family brings proof of address.
3. A student who moves to a shelter across the city has to change schools.
4. Teachers should tell the class when a student is homeless, so classmates can help.
5. A 16-year-old living on their own, without a parent, can enroll themselves in school.

**Answer key:** 1 Agree (doubled up because of lost housing). 2 Disagree (enrollment must be immediate, even without records). 3 Disagree (they can stay in their school of origin when it's in their best interest, with transportation). 4 Disagree (housing status is confidential). 5 Agree (unaccompanied youth are covered and can be enrolled right away).

## 2. Guess the Number (before the video)

Each table writes down a guess on a sticky note:

- How many Maryland students were identified as experiencing homelessness in 2023–24?
- How many of them were in Baltimore City?

Reveal after the video's numbers scene. **Answers:** 20,402 statewide; 5,732 in Baltimore City, more than 1 in 4 of the state total and about 1 in 13 City Schools students (MSDE). Ask: *"What surprised you? What might that look like in a school of 500?"* (About 38 students.)

## 3. Notice · Refer · Protect note-catcher (during the video)

Hand out a three-column sheet. While watching, participants jot one idea in each column:

| Notice | Refer | Protect |
|---|---|---|
| A sign I might see | Who I'd tell, and how | Something I must keep private or never let become a barrier |

Use these notes in Activities 4–7.

## 4. Who Qualifies? Card Sort (after the video)

Give each table the cards below. Sort them into three piles: **Likely eligible — refer**, **Not enough information — refer and let the liaison decide**, and **Likely not eligible**. Stress that staff don't make the final decision; the McKinney-Vento liaison does. When in doubt, refer.

1. Maya's family lost their apartment after a fire. They're staying in a relative's basement.
2. Andre's family lives in a motel because they can't afford a deposit on an apartment.
3. Jasmine, 17, left home after conflict with her parents and is sleeping at friends' houses.
4. The Lee family sold their house and is renting while their new home is built.
5. Carlos and his mom sleep in their car some nights and at a shelter on others.
6. Tiana's family moved in with her grandmother. Tiana mentions "we had to leave our old place."
7. A newly enrolled student has no records and says they're "staying with people."
8. Jordan lives in a trailer with no running water or electricity.

**Answer key:**
- **Likely eligible:** 1 (doubled up after losing housing), 2 (motel for lack of alternatives), 3 (unaccompanied youth), 5 (car and shelter), 8 (inadequate housing).
- **Not enough information, refer:** 6 (the reason for moving matters) and 7 (needs a private conversation with the liaison).
- **Likely not eligible:** 4 (a planned move with fixed, adequate housing).

**Debrief:** which cards caused the most debate? Why is "refer when unsure" the safest rule?

## 5. Barrier Busters (after the video)

Each table gets one situation. In 5 minutes they decide: *What right is at stake? What should happen instead? Who do you contact?* Each table shares a 1-minute answer.

1. The front office tells a mother her son can't start until she brings a lease and immunization records.
2. A student who moved to a shelter across town has missed four days because there's no way to get to school.
3. A student can't go on a field trip because the $25 fee is overdue.
4. A student keeps getting sent to the office for not wearing a uniform.
5. A family and the school disagree about whether the student qualifies. The school says the student must stay home until it's resolved.
6. A student hasn't eaten breakfast because the family hasn't filled out a meal form.

**Answer key:**
1. Immediate enrollment is required, even without records; the liaison helps get them afterward.
2. School of origin with transportation; call the McKinney-Vento liaison right away.
3. Fee waivers are part of the program's services; contact the liaison.
4. Uniforms and clothing are available through the program; avoid discipline for something the student can't control.
5. The student is enrolled and attends while the dispute is resolved.
6. Students experiencing homelessness are automatically eligible for free meals; no form is needed.

## 6. The Right Words: Role-Play (after the video)

In groups of three: one plays a staff member, one plays a student, one observes using the checklist. Rotate roles for each scenario.

- **Scenario A:** A usually on-time 4th grader has been late all week, falling asleep in class, and wearing the same clothes.
- **Scenario B:** A 10th grader mentions they've been "couch surfing" and asks you not to tell anyone.
- **Scenario C:** A parent at pickup says they're "between places right now" and worried about their child changing schools.

**Observer checklist:**
- [ ] Spoke privately, not in front of others
- [ ] Used neutral words ("Where are you staying right now?") rather than "Are you homeless?"
- [ ] Didn't promise secrecy they can't keep, but explained that information is shared only with people who can help
- [ ] Named the next step: the school's McKinney-Vento liaison
- [ ] Didn't make the student or family prove anything

**Debrief:** what phrases worked? What felt awkward, and how could you say it differently?

## 7. School Action Map (closing)

School teams fill in a chart-paper map with four boxes:

1. **Our liaison:** name, room, extension. (Citywide line: 443-642-2424.)
2. **Where students might be noticed:** front office, attendance, nurse, cafeteria, buses, coaches.
3. **Our referral steps:** who tells whom, how quickly, and how it stays confidential.
4. **One barrier we'll remove this month:** for example, a uniform closet, a breakfast grab-and-go, or a front-office enrollment script.

Teams post their maps for a 3-minute gallery walk and leave one sticky-note idea on another team's map.

## 8. 3-2-1 Exit Ticket (closing)

- **3** signs that a student might be experiencing homelessness
- **2** rights a student has under McKinney-Vento
- **1** person at my school I'll contact, and their name

- **Quick quiz** (Kahoot, Mentimeter, or paper): reuse the Agree / Disagree statements and the Guess the Number questions.
- **Timeline match-up:** cards with 1987, 2000, 2001, 2015 and the matching events from the video's history scene.
