## Scene 1: Hook (0:00–0:15)
**On screen:** "Some of your students won't sleep in the same place tonight as they did last night."
**Narration:** Somewhere in your building there is a student who slept in a shelter, a motel, a car, or on a relative's couch last night. Federal law gives that student specific rights, and every one of us has a part in protecting them.

## Scene 2: What is McKinney-Vento? (0:15–0:35)
**On screen:** McKinney-Vento Homeless Assistance Act, Title VII-B (as amended by ESSA)
**Narration:** The McKinney-Vento Act is a federal law. It keeps children and youth who lose their housing enrolled in school, attending, and on track to succeed. In Maryland, MSDE oversees it. Here in Baltimore, City Schools' Office of Homeless Services carries it out.

## Scene 3: The numbers (0:35–1:00)
**On screen (animated counters):**
- Nationally: a record **~1.5 million** students experienced homelessness in 2023–24 (U.S. Dept. of Education / NCHE)
- Maryland: **15,500+** students identified in 2023–24, about 1.7% of enrollment ⚠️
- Baltimore City: **~5,700** students identified in 2023–24, about **1 in 13** City Schools students ⚠️
- Baltimore City alone accounts for **about 1 in 3** of Maryland's identified students ⚠️

**Narration:** Across the country, a record one and a half million students experienced homelessness in 2023–24. In Maryland, more than fifteen thousand were identified. About fifty-seven hundred of them were here in Baltimore City. That is roughly one in thirteen of our students, and about a third of all the identified students in the state.

## Scene 4: Who qualifies? (1:00–1:30)
**On screen (icon grid):** A student lacks a **fixed, regular, and adequate** nighttime residence, for example:
- Sharing housing with others because of loss of housing or economic hardship ("doubled-up")
- Living in a motel or hotel, trailer park, or campground for lack of alternatives
- Living in an emergency or transitional shelter
- Living in a car, park, abandoned building, bus or train station, or substandard housing
- Unaccompanied youth: not in the physical custody of a parent or guardian

**Narration:** Homelessness doesn't always look the way we expect. Most identified students are "doubled-up," staying with friends or relatives because they lost their housing. Others live in motels, shelters, cars, or places not meant for people to live. Young people living on their own, without a parent or guardian, are covered too.

## Scene 5: Students' rights (1:30–2:05)
**On screen (checklist):**
- ✅ **Immediate enrollment**, even without records, proof of residency, immunizations, or guardianship papers
- ✅ Stay in the **school of origin** when it's in the student's best interest
- ✅ **Transportation** to the school of origin
- ✅ **Free school meals** right away
- ✅ Full access to programs and activities: Title I, special education, extracurriculars
- ✅ **Enroll during any dispute** while it is being resolved

**Narration:** Students experiencing homelessness must be enrolled immediately, even if they don't have records, proof of address, or immunization papers. They can stay in their school of origin when that is in their best interest, and City Schools provides transportation to get them there. They are automatically eligible for free meals. They have full access to every program and activity. If there is a disagreement about enrollment, the student is enrolled while it is being resolved.

## Scene 6: What City Schools provides (2:05–2:20)
**On screen:** Uniforms and clothing · School supplies and backpacks · Transportation support · Laundry support · Fee waivers · Referrals to community partners
**Narration:** Through the Office of Homeless Services, City Schools helps families with uniforms and clothing, school supplies, transportation, laundry support, fee waivers, and connections to community resources.

## Scene 7: Your role (2:20–2:45)
**On screen:** **Notice → Refer → Protect**
1. **Notice** the signs: address changes, attendance gaps, missing supplies, fatigue, or mentions of "staying with" someone
2. **Refer** to your school-based homeless liaison (often the social worker or counselor), who works with the Office of Homeless Services
3. **Protect** privacy. A student's housing status is a confidential education record under FERPA, so never discuss it in front of peers
4. **Never** delay enrollment or services while waiting for documents

**Narration:** Your part comes down to three words: notice, refer, protect. Notice the signs. Refer the student to your school's homeless liaison. Protect the student's privacy, because housing status is confidential. And never let missing paperwork become a barrier.
